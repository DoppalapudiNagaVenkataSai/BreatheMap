About BreatheMap

BreatheMap helps urban residents and families reduce how much air pollution they personally breathe in by deciding when to go out, how to travel, and which route to take.

1. Personal Exposure vs AQI

An AQI number alone does not reflect personal intake. Your inhaled pollution depends on the route chosen, trip duration, breathing rate, microenvironment (in-vehicle vs pedestrian), and departure timing.

2. Inhaled Dose Formula

The comparative PM2.5 dose (µg) is modelled across route segments:

Dose = Σ C_i(t_i) × F_mode × F_road,i × V_mode × Δt_i

Where V_mode is ventilation rate (m³/h), F_mode is microenvironment filtering, and F_road is road proximity.

3. Indicative CPCB NAQI

In India, BreatheMap defaults to an indicative National Air Quality Index (NAQI) calculated from Open-Meteo modelled pollutant data. It is not an official CPCB monitoring station reading.

4. Privacy and AI Transparency

BreatheMap is anonymous: no accounts, no cookies for tracking, and raw IP addresses are never stored (only daily HMAC hashes). Community reports expire automatically. When using the AI Assistant, messages are processed via the Google Gemini free tier (which Google may use to improve products) — please do not share personal details.

5. Data Sources & Attributions

  • Air quality & weather: Open-Meteo / CAMS (CC BY 4.0)
  • Routing: openrouteservice.org & FOSSGIS OSRM / OpenStreetMap contributors
  • Geocoding & street lighting tags: Nominatim & Overpass API / OpenStreetMap contributors
  • Map tiles: © OpenStreetMap contributors
  • AI explanations: Google Gemini (gemini-3.8-flash)

6. Not Medical Advice

BreatheMap is a comparative decision-support tool, not a medical device. It does not diagnose, prescribe, or provide medical advice. For health concerns, consult a doctor; in an emergency, call 112 (in India) or your local emergency number.
